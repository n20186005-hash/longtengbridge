## What is the north broken bridge?

The northern ruins are the red-brick remains most visitors first see when they arrive at Longteng Bridge. Tall piers, partial arches and protective reinforcement are concentrated around the main visitor area, suitable for short stops, photography and interpretation panels.

## Where is the south broken bridge?

The southern remains lie across the valley on the south side of the original Yutengping Bridge. They are not just around a corner within the north-side visitor area: reaching their viewing route requires a different approach or an appropriate rail-bike itinerary.

The [Miaoli tourism portal](https://miaolitravel.net/article.aspx?sNo=04004516) introduces both sections together; always distinguish their actual visitor access points when planning.

## How were the two sections once connected?

The original railway bridge crossed the valley using brick piers, arches and other bridge structures. The devastating 1935 earthquake damaged it severely. Railway traffic later moved onto the newly built Yutengping Iron Bridge to the west, while fragments of the old bridge remained on both sides of the valley.

## Which route should you consider for the south bridge?

Start by checking Old Mountain Line Rail Bike Route A. Its published route description includes the iron bridge, a distant view of the northern remains and the southern broken bridge. Confirm stops, commentary and available departures directly with the operator.

## Is it incomplete to see only the north section?

Not at all. The north side already conveys the size of the brick arches and the earthquake damage. For first-time visitors, people with little time or anyone who walks slowly, it is the core experience.

The south side is an optional deeper exploration for those interested in history and railway routes.

## How can you identify each part in photos?

- Huge consecutive brick arches next to the main recreation area: usually the north section.
- Brick remains viewed from the iron bridge or railway route: potentially a distant view of the north.
- Ruins in a more secluded valley with a guided stop: likely the south section.

## Planning Route C

The Route C experience can differ from other lines, and details may change. Use the operator's current information rather than relying on an old itinerary.

## Before departure

Confirm service times, starting and ending stations, travel direction, duration and stops. Then decide whether you also want a ground-level visit to the north section of Longteng Bridge.

## Choosing whether to book

If your only goal is to view the red-brick ruins, a paid rail bike ride may not be needed. Choose based on the scenery you want and the cost of transportation.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I freely swap between Routes A, B and C?** Do not assume so. Changes and refunds follow your ticket's conditions.

**Do the ruins require a rail bike ticket?** No; outdoor viewing and the paid ride are separate activities.

**Where should I navigate?** This page concerns the **Old Mountain Line Rail Bike**. Verify the correct search result and actual station on your ticket; no fixed Place ID is supplied here. Navigate separately to the bridge monument if desired.

## How Route C differs

Lettered rail bike routes are more than fare names: they can represent different starting points, sections of track or sightseeing activities. Route versions can change, so review the latest official diagram.

## Three checks before buying

First confirm both stations, then the stops and guided content, and finally the timetable and return arrangements. Visitors interested in close views of the red-brick arches should determine whether a separate monument visit is necessary.

## Photographing the tunnels

The mountain tunnels contrast dark interiors with bright bridges. Enable a phone's high-dynamic-range setting if useful, keep cameras secured while moving, and avoid strong lights that disturb other passengers inside tunnels.

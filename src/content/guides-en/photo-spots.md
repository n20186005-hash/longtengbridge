## How do you take the classic front-facing photo?

From the main viewpoint, make the series of arches the subject and keep a little foreground and forest in the frame. A person helps viewers understand the scale; wait for a quieter moment if you prefer a clean composition.

## Why do side views tell a stronger story?

From the side, the thickness of the arches, the location of the break and vegetation reclaiming the structure become more visible. This angle shows the actual historical condition rather than suggesting a symmetrical, intact bridge.

## How can you photograph both bridges together?

Within the public viewing area, find a position showing both the brick arches and the steel girder bridge behind. The former belongs to the original 1907 bridge; the latter is the post-earthquake replacement. Their shared frame is a visual timeline.

## What weather is best?

Overcast skies soften the contrast between brick and forest, revealing details more evenly. Sunshine creates stronger color and shadows. After rain, the brick may look darker, but surfaces can be slippery: safety takes priority over framing.

## Three smartphone tips

1. Clean the lens before switching to wide angle.
2. Turn on the grid to keep tall piers from tilting dramatically.
3. At one position, capture a wide scene, a medium view and a close-up of the brickwork.

## Which locations are off limits?

Never enter railway tracks, cross barriers, climb the remains or stand in the roadway for an empty shot. Drone use must follow local and aviation rules and respect other visitors and heritage protection.

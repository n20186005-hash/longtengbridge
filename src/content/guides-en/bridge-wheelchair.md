## Planning a visit with limited mobility

For wheelchairs, walkers and visitors with knee discomfort, focus first on an easy-to-reach view of the ruins rather than trying every photo location.

## Before arriving

Check usable parking and passenger drop-off points. Companions should assess gradients, stairs, loose gravel and slippery ground on site before deciding whether to continue.

## What to expect on site

Access facilities can change, so an entirely step-free route cannot be promised. Contact the site manager for the most reliable current advice.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can a wheelchair reach every part of the bridge?** This cannot be guaranteed. Prioritize routes marked as accessible on site.

**Is admission charged?** Outdoor monument viewing and the paid rail bike experience are separate activities.

**Where should I navigate?** Use [Longteng Bridge](https://maps.app.goo.gl/55jN8AsmsDdr5xGs5) for the monument, and the booked station for a rail bike ride.

## Heritage terrain and accessible sightseeing

The hilly setting of Yutengping Bridge has different grades and surfaces. Some viewing angles involve stairs, slopes or gravel, so do not assume a wheelchair can reach every section.

## Choosing a safer route

Ask site management which viewpoints and parking locations are easiest to access. On arrival, assess the surface and slope before continuing; there is no need to enter narrow paths simply to approach the structure.

## Companions and photography

A zoom lens can capture the full arches without extensive movement. Companions should help passengers board or leave vehicles at a safe location, never by stopping on a bend.

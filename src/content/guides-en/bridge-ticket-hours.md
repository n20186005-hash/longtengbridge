## How do tickets and visiting hours work?

The outdoor Longteng Bridge ruins and the Old Mountain Line Rail Bike are separate experiences. Be clear which one you intend to visit before looking up prices or schedules.

## What to check ahead of time

Generally, viewing the monument from open outdoor areas does not require a rail bike ticket. Individual areas may nevertheless close temporarily for weather, maintenance or events.

## How the paid activity differs

The rail bike uses reservations or paid tickets. Operating dates, session times, eligibility rules, prices and service changes should be confirmed directly with the operator.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I visit at night?** An outdoor location is not necessarily safe or accessible at every hour. Check lighting and current restrictions on site.

**Must I purchase a ticket to see the bridge?** Ordinary outdoor viewing and the ticketed rail bike are distinct activities.

**Where do I navigate?** [Longteng Bridge on Google Maps](https://maps.app.goo.gl/55jN8AsmsDdr5xGs5) is for the historic ruins; follow the station on your reservation for the rail bike.

## Free monument viewing and paid activities

The open viewing areas around Longteng Bridge and the Old Mountain Line Rail Bike do not share one ticketing system. Visitors can appreciate the brick arches from permitted places; rail bike access depends on operating days, departure sessions and booking terms.

## How long to allow

For the monument alone, plan roughly 30 to 60 minutes, depending on travel, photography and reading information panels. For a paid ride, add check-in, waiting and transfers between stations.

## Nighttime and weather restrictions

Outdoor heritage sites do not promise unrestricted access at all times. Lighting, construction restrictions and wet paths may affect your visit. Follow the most recent on-site notices.

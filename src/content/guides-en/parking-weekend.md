## Planning weekend parking

Parking patterns on weekends and consecutive holidays can be very different from weekdays. First identify whether you are going to the heritage monument or the rail bike.

## Before departing

Set the map destination to the Longteng Bridge heritage area for sightseeing. For a booked rail bike ride, use the check-in station on your reservation. Longteng Station and Longteng Bridge are not synonymous.

## Arriving on the hillside road

Coming earlier reduces the chance of congestion but cannot guarantee parking. Narrow bends and residents' entrances along Mia 49 must remain clear, even where the shoulder looks wide.

## Frequently asked questions

**What about visiting with children on a holiday?** Park and unload only at legal, safe locations, then walk together away from the traffic. Never let passengers out on a bend.

**Does the monument require a ticket?** General outdoor viewing is different from the charged Old Mountain Line Rail Bike.

**What is the correct destination?** [Longteng Bridge on Google Maps](https://maps.app.goo.gl/55jN8AsmsDdr5xGs5) is the historic site; follow your ticket for rail bike check-in.

## Recognizing proper parking on busy days

The ruins sit beside mountain roads where parking depends on narrow lanes and visitor numbers. Find a clearly marked parking area before identifying the pedestrian entrance to the brick-arch viewpoint. On holidays, do not confuse the remaining driving distance on a map with the time needed to park and unload.

## From the car to the arches

Choose a group meeting point away from moving cars. Follow signs to the open viewpoint, take in the broad shape of the arches and read the interpretation boards. Return to your vehicle on permitted paths, without crossing private land or barriers.

## Photos and alternate plans

A wide lens can frame the brick bridge and hills together. If parking is full, follow lawful traffic guidance and change the order of stops—for example, going to Shengxing Station or another open site first. Never stop on a blind bend for a quick photograph.

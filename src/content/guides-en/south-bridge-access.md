## How to visit the southern remains

The southern part of Longteng Bridge is not reached in the same way as its main northern viewpoint. Identify the part you hope to see before you travel.

## Plan access before departure

The northern remains lend themselves to a short viewpoint visit. For the south, review Old Mountain Line Rail Bike routes and their current stop arrangements rather than assuming both ends share one entrance.

## On-site restrictions

Check the operator's published route for viewing and stops. Never enter an unopened railway section independently.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I see the south without a rail bike ride?** Different views may exist, but do not rely on unauthorized shortcuts or private land.

**Do I need a ticket to see Longteng Bridge?** General northern outdoor viewing is separate from the paid rail bike ride.

## Understanding the north–south relationship

The broken bridge is not a continuous walkway connecting north and south. Southern views differ from the main entrance, and some travelers learn about this part through the Old Mountain Line Rail Bike.

## Before seeking the southern arches

Check whether your current rail bike route includes a southern viewpoint and verify its starting and check-in stations. If not riding, observe only from legally open areas rather than trying to cross restricted railway land.

## Photography priorities

Compare the surviving structure with the surrounding valley. Views differ by date, route and stopping location, and no single viewpoint can guarantee a photograph of every span.
